Ratan

I study how nuclei are forged in stars — thermonuclear reaction rates, nuclear level densities, and the reaction networks that reproduce solar and stellar isotopic abundances, from the γ‑process to the r‑process.

Research profile

I work on the nuclear physics that underlies stellar and explosive nucleosynthesis: computing thermonuclear reaction rates, modelling nuclear level densities and γ‑ray strength functions, and running reaction‑network calculations that connect microscopic nuclear inputs to the isotopic abundances we observe in stars, meteorites, and the solar system. Much of my work sits at the interface of nuclear structure theory, statistical (Hauser–Feshbach) reaction theory, and astrophysical modelling.

Research interests

↑ top

Nucleosynthesis reaction networks

  • γ‑process nucleosynthesis reaction networks

    Photodisintegration‑driven network calculations for the proton‑rich p‑nuclei — tackling the numerical stiffness of single‑zone integration with damped Picard iteration and Structure‑of‑Arrays optimizations, and running WinNet and NuGrid ppn for benchmark comparisons.

  • Reproducing published thermonuclear rates

    Python implementations reproducing literature rate calculations — for example the 26P(p,γ)27S and 42Ti(p,γ)43V rates — including a corrected reverse‑rate formula and a lightweight one‑zone driver for sanity checks against full network codes.

  • s‑process and r‑process nucleosynthesis

    Broader interest in slow and rapid neutron‑capture nucleosynthesis and how reaction‑rate uncertainties propagate into predicted isotopic abundance patterns.

Nuclear structure inputs

  • Nuclear level density modelling

    Back‑Shifted Fermi Gas, Constant Temperature, and Generalised Superfluid model descriptions of the nuclear level density, and how well they extrapolate beyond the discrete‑level region.

  • Bayesian uncertainty quantification

    MCMC‑based (emcee) uncertainty quantification for level‑density model parameters — reproducing and auditing published fits (including for 105Pd, 106Pd, 105Cd, 106Cd, 127Te, and 147Nd).

  • Statistical (Hauser–Feshbach) reaction theory

    Combining level density, γ‑ray strength function (SLO/GLO/EGLO), and optical‑potential inputs into cross‑section and astrophysical rate calculators, with Total Monte Carlo propagation of input uncertainties to the final rate.

    $$\sigma_{ab}(E) \propto \sum_{J,\pi} (2J+1)\,\frac{T_a(E,J,\pi)\,T_b(E,J,\pi)}{T_{\text{tot}}(E,J,\pi)}$$

Computational tools

Day‑to‑day work draws on TALYS, MESA, REACLIB, WinNet, and NuGrid ppn for nuclear‑astrophysics modelling, alongside a Python stack (emcee, scipy ODE solvers, custom Structure‑of‑Arrays network integrators) for method development and rapid prototyping.

Project ideas

↑ top

Open project and thesis ideas for students, scoped from a semester to a full thesis.

Nucleosynthesis & reaction networks

  • Sensitivity study of the γ‑process network to individual rate uncertainties

    Vary one reaction rate at a time (or in correlated groups) through the existing network solver and rank which rates most affect final p‑nuclei abundances.

    Scope: one semester · Skills: Python, ODE integration

  • Benchmarking a one‑zone solver against WinNet / NuGrid ppn

    Run a small reaction network through the custom Picard‑iteration solver and through WinNet or ppn, and characterise where and why the results diverge.

    Scope: one semester · Skills: Fortran/Python, numerical methods

Nuclear structure & statistical models

  • Extending Bayesian UQ from level densities to combined NLD + GSF fits

    Extend the existing emcee‑based level‑density UQ pipeline to jointly fit level density and γ‑ray strength function parameters, and compare posterior correlations.

    Scope: full thesis · Skills: Python, emcee/MCMC, Bayesian statistics

  • Model comparison for nuclear level density near shell closures

    Compare BSFG, CT, and GSM fits for nuclei near a shell closure using Bayes factors or information criteria rather than chi‑square alone.

    Scope: one semester · Skills: Python, statistics

  • Total Monte Carlo uncertainty propagation for a Hauser–Feshbach rate calculator

    Wire the existing NLD + GSF + optical‑potential calculator into a Total Monte Carlo loop that propagates input‑model uncertainty to a final rate with a confidence band.

    Scope: one semester · Skills: Python, statistics, reaction theory

Tools & outreach

  • Public γ‑ray strength function repository web app

    Turn a local GSF dataset into a small searchable web application with interactive SLO/GLO/EGLO model overlays.

    Scope: one semester · Skills: web development, nuclear data formats

  • Interactive teaching module: from nuclear inputs to stellar abundances

    A guided page that lets a student change a level‑density or rate parameter and see the effect ripple through to a final abundance pattern.

    Scope: one semester · Skills: HTML/JS/canvas, physics communication

These are starting points, not fixed assignments — if you have your own idea adjacent to reaction rates, nucleosynthesis, or nuclear structure modelling, get in touch and we can scope something together.
